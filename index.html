Why it is two transactions

The first burns your KLANKO and writes down your address and the block you're in. Nothing is minted by it. The second draws your BitPunk, using the hash of the block after your commit as the entropy.

That hash does not exist while you are signing the first transaction. If the draw were decided inside the transaction that pays, anyone could simulate it, inspect the punk, and back out when it was a common one. Splitting the two takes that away from everybody.

How long you have

Your commitment is anchored to a block. After one block you can reveal; the window to draw stays open for a while, so you don't lose your burn if you take your time. If it closes, re-anchoring just moves it to the current block for gas — nothing is paid twice, and the KLANKO stays burned.

Which punk you get

Token ids come out of a lazy Fisher-Yates shuffle. The draw indexes into whatever ids are still unclaimed, no id can come out twice, and the pick stays uniform over the remainder all the way down to the last one.
